# GeoBlock: the project so far

*Development record through 5 October 2026. This page describes experiments and product decisions; it is not a claim that the general landmark-to-video pipeline is finished.*

## The idea

GeoBlock should let someone choose a landmark they care about, find open geographic data good enough to represent it, plan a camera move through a 3D blocking scene, and turn that scene into a finished video with MiniMax H3. The 3D render supplies spatial layout and motion; photographs or other licensed imagery supply place identity; separate style references can guide the final look. The intended product does **not** ship a menu of fixed landmarks. Worm's Head and Woodside Library are development fixtures used to learn what a general pipeline must check.

The [original implementation plan](GeoBlock_Implementation_Plan.md) proposed a matched comparison of coarse global terrain and local LiDAR. That plan is useful background, but it predates the Blender and H3 experiments. The [README](../README.md) and [pipeline status](programmatic-flyover.md) describe the present implementation.

## From terrain to a recognisable flyover

The first site was Worm's Head at Rhossili. A 30 m global terrain crop and a 1 m Welsh LiDAR crop made the resolution problem concrete: a coarse terrain model can describe broad relief but cannot recover a narrow tidal crossing or a cliff edge missing from its cells. The first close crop also made the promontory appear detached because it omitted the causeway and headland. The scene was widened to include the connected landform, and the camera was moved to a lookout at the headland edge. A Blender flyover then travelled out over the sea, past the outer head, turned, and returned. [The README](../README.md#cli) gives the preparation and render commands.

Adding a sea plane exposed a different problem. A raster height near zero is not an observed tide, and a terrain model's vertical datum is not automatically the water surface. The Welsh LiDAR's delivered vertical reference has not been confirmed for this tile, so the current sea height is an illustrative staging value. The project records source height frames, missing cells and transformation grids rather than claiming a certified shoreline or sea level. The [source check](source-check.md) contains the datum evidence and the unresolved gate.

The first local H3 Ref2VA test showed that a clay 3D flyover plus real place images could produce a recognisable Worm's Head flyover. Subsequent takes slowed the route, requested moving breakers rather than a static white seam, and widened the turn to reduce a visible jump. The fixed take still changes viewpoint near its end, so the review version trims that portion. This established the basic motion-reference approach, **not** a fully stable final shot. The renders and model weights remain local, outside GitHub; the scripts and reference-selection method are in this repository.

## Woodside revealed the building problem

The second fixture moved to Woodside Library, California, to test an address-centred flyover with buildings. USGS 3DEP supplied bare-earth terrain and NAIP supplied aerial place imagery. An aerial drape made the roads and vegetation recognisable, but roofs and trees were flat on the terrain. Zooming closer exposed the lack of building depth. A simplified flat guide sometimes helped the requested clear-line look, yet H3 could still invent a map pin or collapse into map-like colour patches. Prompting and style stills did not reliably override the appearance of a flat or photographic motion reference. The [Woodside experiment log](woodside-library-test.md) records these comparisons and their limits.

The next control used USGS LiDAR point returns to estimate height inside checked OpenStreetMap building footprints. For a 200 m neighbourhood, 52 of 66 footprints passed the roof-return checks; the rest were omitted. The result provided separate building volumes and useful parallax, and a tighter opening frame plus a 220 m aerial crop improved nearby roof and facade legibility in an H3 test. Those blocks still have flat, inferred roofs: **measured height is not measured building shape**. The current Woodside material is an experimental height-and-footprint fixture, not an eligible architecture-led product scene.

This led to two geometry gates. A natural-landform shot needs measured relief at its target. A building- or architecture-led shot needs validated roof or mesh geometry for the target **and** the buildings visible along the camera path, with terrain aligned underneath. A DTM, aerial photograph, 2D footprint, or height-only extrusion cannot by itself satisfy the building-shape gate. The [map-resource survey](map-resource-survey.md#building-shape-is-a-separate-quality-gate) describes candidate LoD2/city-mesh sources and the checks each selected tile still needs.

## Sources, imagery and rendering

The research broadened from a single map to distinct scene layers: land DTM, above-ground detail, building meshes, bathymetry, imagery, vectors and a separately chosen sea surface. A catalog entry can tell GeoBlock where to look, but not whether every visible building exists or a specific landmark is modelled. The [Open 3D City Model Catalog](https://catalog.open3d.city/), Hamburg, Helsinki and 3DBAG are useful leads; none becomes a qualified shot merely by appearing in a list. The selected tile must pass coverage, shape, date, licence and alignment checks. [The survey](map-resource-survey.md) also records why extracted Apple/Google photogrammetry tiles are not a default reusable input; exporter repositories do not provide rights to the map content.

Place references are collected programmatically where possible. The Worm's Head collector searches Wikimedia Commons, records each chosen file's author, licence, source page and hash, and keeps the downloaded photos local. The Woodside scripts fetch public-domain USGS NAIP crops and record their acquisition details. Two original generated clear-line images serve as style references; they are not geographic evidence. The renderer is replaceable in principle, but Blender remains the working blocking path. A quick [forge3d trial](map-resource-survey.md#renderer-lead-forge3d) rendered terrain checkpoints rapidly while exposing coastline and sea-fill problems, so it has not replaced Blender for the current shot.

## The ComfyUI and H3 boundary

The GIS compiler produces terrain assets and provenance outside ComfyUI. Blender turns qualified geometry into a camera-control video. ComfyUI nodes then expose place, scene, shot and checked references; a separate H3 graph uses the control video and images. This separation keeps source acquisition and GIS dependencies out of the video model's prompt. It also makes the work repeatable: a stage can write source, licence, bounds, datum, hashes and settings to a manifest. [The reproduction guide](REPRODUCING.md) explains what is in GitHub and which large inputs must be acquired separately.

MiniMax H3 runs locally with the Comfy-Org pruned INT8 Ref2VA checkpoint on the test workstation. The optional [Veda ComfyUI node](https://github.com/veda-sparse/Veda-on-ComfyUI) has been installed and wired into separately named graph variants, keeping reference attention dense. Its RTX 4090 backend self-tests passed, but a same-seed full H3 render has **not** yet established speed or visual fidelity for GeoBlock's 12-step, 864 × 480 Ref2VA settings. Veda is an experimental switch, not a proven default. [Pipeline notes](programmatic-flyover.md#optional-veda-h3-attention) record the configuration.

## From example places to user choice

The first ComfyUI graph could only resolve the two prepared names. That was useful for testing, but wrong for the product. The current [landmark-selection canvas](../examples/landmark_selection_comfy.json) accepts a user's search, displays numbered results with coordinates and OpenStreetMap feature IDs, and lets the user choose the exact result and coverage radius. A live test selected the Eiffel Tower from several similarly named results. The chosen place is passed forward with **3D status unchecked**. The old fixture resolver and prepared-scene node remain only for reproducing the tests and refuse to substitute a nearby demo scene for a newly searched place. [Landmark selection](landmark-selection.md) documents the interface and search-provider limits.

This is the present product boundary: a user can choose a landmark, but GeoBlock cannot yet fetch, validate and render an arbitrary chosen landmark end to end. The next vertical slice should take a user-chosen place in a region with open building meshes, intersect its requested area and intended camera corridor with candidate model tiles, inspect the actual target and visible building geometry, align it with measured terrain, and only then enable a shot. The same pipeline should support natural landforms where terrain relief is the subject. Automated checks can reject missing height, missing shapes, gaps and inconsistent datums; human review is still needed for geographic faithfulness, motion continuity and visual style.
